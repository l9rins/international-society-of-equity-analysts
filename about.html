Heritage

Our History & Heritage

From inception to institutional maturity — the milestones that shaped the Society.

2023

Formation & Founding Charter

A group of investment practitioners convened to address the growing inconsistency in analytical standards across jurisdictions. The founding Charter was first drafted and the beginnings of the Society's institutional framework was formulated.

2024

Governance Structure & Ethical Framework

A provisional form of the Governing Council was established to shape the Society’s initial governance architecture. This early Council drafted the Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct.

2025

Formation of the Governing Council

The Governing Council was formally established as the Society’s principal decisionmaking body. Its constitution marked the transition from the initial founding group to a fully authorised governance authority.

2025

CEA Program Design & Curriculum Development

The Education and Certification Committee commenced the design of the Certified Equity Analyst program, establishing the examination framework, curriculum standards and continuing professional development requirements.

2025

Membership Launch & Admission

The Society opened membership applications across all grades and admitted its inaugural cohort of members. Their admission marked the beginning of a formal professional community built around shared standards of practice, ethics and analytical discipline.

2025

Global Chapter Network Begins

The first regional chapters were established, providing a structured presence for the Society in its initial international market. These chapters served as the model for how future regional representation would be introduced as the profession matured across jurisdictions.

2026

Research Publication & Institutional Partnerships

The International Journal of Equity Analysis published its inaugural issue. Strategic partnerships were initiated with leading institutional investors, regulatory bodies and academic affiliates across multiple countries.

2026

Global Recognition & Continued Expansion

The Society now represents members on an international basis. Its international footprint continues to widen as regulators, universities and professional bodies recognise the value of a common ethical and analytical framework.

Purpose

Mission, Vision & Global Values

Our vision is to become the world's leading professional institution representing equity analysts and investment research professionals by establishing the global benchmark for excellence, integrity and professional recognition in equity analysis.

Our mission is to advance and elevate the profession of equity analysis by establishing internationally recognised standards, promoting independent and rigorous analytical judgement, supporting lifelong professional development, fostering ethical conduct, recognising professional excellence, and building a global community of equity analysts.

Our Core Values

  • Independence of Thought

    Analysts must form conclusions free from commercial pressure or institutional bias.

  • Analytical Discipline

    Methods must be coherent, evidence-based and proportionate to the decisions they inform.

  • Ethical Responsibility

    The consequences of poor judgement extend beyond individual firms into the broader financial system.

  • Professionalism

    Members conduct themselves with discretion, respect and a clear understanding of their influence.

Leadership

Governing Council & Executive Secretariat

The Governing Council serves as the Society's highest governing authority and is responsible for safeguarding the institution's independence, credibility and long-term direction. It is structured to reflect the standards expected of senior practitioners – those who demonstrate sustained professional judgement, ethical resilience and a commitment to the advancement of equity analysis.

The Council oversees the Society's strategic priorities, approves major policy positions and maintains accountability across all credentialing, disciplinary and governance functions. The role of the Council is to protect the integrity of the profession, even when doing so requires decisions that are not commercially convenient.

Supporting the Council is the Executive Secretariat, structured to reflect the experience and judgement expected of senior practitioners engaged in the Society's work. The Executive Secretariat functions as the operational and strategic conduit between the Council's directives and the Society's broader activities, ensuring that initiatives are executed with discipline and coherence.

The Governing Council and the Executive Secretariat form a governance architecture designed to be stable, principled and professionally demanding. Their combined oversight ensures that the Society remains an institution defined by integrity rather than convenience, and by long-term stewardship rather than short-term visibility.

Advisory Structure

Global Standing Committees

The Global Standing Committees form an important part of the Society's institutional architecture, providing a structured channel through which specialist judgement can be applied to policy, credentialing, research and professional standards. Each committee operates with a defined mandate.

Some focus on curriculum and examination standards, ensuring that the Certified Equity Analyst designation reflects contemporary analytical practice. Others concentrate on regulatory developments, providing structured input on emerging disclosure requirements. Additional committees oversee research priorities, professional ethics, continuing development requirements and the recognition of advanced standing for experienced practitioners.

Committee members are selected for their professional record, independence of judgement and willingness to contribute to the long-term integrity of the profession. Their work directly influences how standards are set, how examinations evolve, how disciplinary matters are assessed and how the Society positions itself in global policy discussions.

Global Presence

Global Chapters & Regional Offices

The Society's global orientation reflects a deliberate commitment to maintaining professional standards across jurisdictions while recognising the distinct regulatory, cultural and market characteristics that shape different financial centres.

Chapters are led by practitioners who understand both local market dynamics and the expectations of an international professional body. Their role is custodial rather than administrative, ensuring that credentialing, continuing development, disciplinary processes and member engagement are executed with consistency and integrity.

The Society's approach to global expansion is intentionally selective. Formal representation is introduced only where professional need, regulatory alignment and the presence of practitioners capable of upholding the Society's standards justify it.

Transparency

Annual Reports & Governance Disclosures

The Society's annual reporting framework is designed to provide members, regulators and institutional stakeholders with a clear view of how the organisation exercises its mandate. Our annual reports are formal records of stewardship, professional standards oversight and the operational decisions that shape the institution's direction.

Governance disclosures detail the structure and responsibilities of the Governing Council, the Executive Secretariat and the Global Standing Committees, along with the processes through which decisions are made, reviewed and escalated. This transparency is intentional – the Society applies the same expectations to itself that it demands of its members.

Financial reporting is conducted with equal seriousness, providing detailed accounts of revenue sources, credentialing program expenditures, research funding, chapter operations and long-term capital commitments.

Collaboration

Strategic Partnerships & Academic Affiliates

The Society’s approach to external engagement is shaped by a deliberate commitment to strengthening the profession’s intellectual foundations.

As part of this framework, the Society has defined a model for strategic partnerships that encompasses regulatory bodies, institutional investors, research organisations and professional associations whose objectives align with the advancement of equity analysis. These relationships will be introduced selectively, consistent with the Society’s standards and longterm mandate.

Academic affiliations form a complementary pillar of this engagement framework. The Society’s academic model is designed to work with universities, business schools and research institutes that maintain rigorous finance programs and demonstrate a sustained commitment to empirical research. These affiliations will support curriculum development, examination design and the advancement of analytical methodologies as they are progressively established.

Academic partners, once formally appointed, will contribute to peerreview processes, participate in advisory structures and support publication through the International Journal of Equity Analysis. Their involvement will ensure that credentialing and professional development programs remain grounded in evidence rather than convention.

Governance Policy

Workplace Health, Safety and Wellbeing Policy

International Society of Equity Analysts

Version: 1.2 Approved: 1 July 2025 Last Updated: 1 July 2026 Administered by: Committee
This document forms part of the official governance framework of the International Society of Equity Analysts (ISEA) and should be read together with the related documents listed above.

Purpose

The International Society of Equity Analysts (“ISEA”) is committed to providing and maintaining a safe, healthy environment for everyone who works for, volunteers with, contracts with, or participates in the activities of ISEA, wherever in the world those activities take place.

As a large, multinational professional association, ISEA maintains offices, employs staff, engages contractors and volunteers, and organises conferences, examinations and chapter events across many jurisdictions. Each of these settings carries distinct health and safety considerations, and ISEA recognises its responsibility to identify, assess and manage the risks associated with each.

This Workplace Health, Safety and Wellbeing Policy (“Policy”) sets out ISEA’s commitment to protecting the physical and psychological health, safety and wellbeing of its employees, offices, event and conference participants, volunteers and contractors, and establishes the principles, responsibilities and processes by which that commitment is implemented.

ISEA regards health, safety and wellbeing not merely as a compliance obligation, but as a fundamental element of responsible organisational governance and as a precondition for a professional community in which people can do their best work and participate fully in ISEA’s mission.

This Policy operates alongside, and should be read together with, ISEA’s Ethics Policy, Equality, Diversity and Inclusion Policy, Risk Management Policy and other applicable ISEA policies and governance requirements.

Scope and Application

This Policy applies to:

  • employees of ISEA, wherever based, including those working from an ISEA office, remotely, or on a hybrid basis;
  • the Secretary-General and members of the Executive Secretariat;
  • Governing Council members, officers and committee members, in connection with ISEA governance activities;
  • volunteers, examiners, assessors, mentors, speakers and other representatives acting on behalf of ISEA;
  • contractors, consultants, secondees and agency staff engaged by ISEA;
  • ISEA Members and other attendees participating in ISEA-organised or ISEA-sanctioned conferences, examinations, chapter meetings and events; and
  • visitors to ISEA offices and premises.

This Policy applies to ISEA offices and premises, ISEA-organised or ISEA-sanctioned events, conferences and examinations, business travel undertaken in connection with ISEA activities, and, in relation to psychological health and wellbeing, to ISEA-related conduct occurring through digital platforms and remote working arrangements.

Because ISEA operates in many jurisdictions, this Policy is intended to establish a consistent minimum standard across ISEA’s global operations, reflecting internationally recognised occupational health and safety principles. Where local law imposes a higher or more specific standard than this Policy in the jurisdiction in which an ISEA office, event or activity is located, the local legal standard shall apply in addition to this Policy. Nothing in this Policy is intended to require, or to excuse compliance with, conduct that would breach applicable local law.

This Policy does not form part of, and does not vary, any contract of employment or engagement. Employment-related occupational health and safety matters will additionally be governed by applicable local law and any separate ISEA human resources policies and procedures.

Definitions

For the purposes of this Policy:

“Hazard” means a source or situation with the potential to cause harm to a person’s health or safety, whether physical or psychological.

“Risk” means the likelihood and potential severity of harm arising from exposure to a Hazard.

“Incident” means an unplanned event that resulted in, or had the potential to result in, injury, illness, property damage or other loss, including a “near miss” where no actual harm occurred.

“Duty of Care” means ISEA’s responsibility to take reasonable steps to protect the health, safety and wellbeing of persons affected by its activities.

“Workplace” means any location where ISEA-related work or activity is performed, including ISEA offices, remote and home working locations, event and conference venues, and locations visited in the course of ISEA-related business travel.

“Wellbeing” means a person’s physical, psychological and social state of being, encompassing both the absence of harm and the presence of factors that support health, resilience and a positive working environment.

“Lone Working” means work carried out by a person without close or direct supervision, or without another person present, whether at an ISEA office, a remote location, or in the course of business travel.

“Fitness for Role” means a person’s physical and psychological capacity to safely and effectively perform the specific duties of their role or activity.

“HSW Committee” means the Health, Safety and Wellbeing Committee established under Section 5 of this Policy.

Policy Statement and Commitment

ISEA is committed to:

  • providing and maintaining a safe and healthy working environment across its offices, events and other activities, so far as is reasonably practicable;
  • identifying, assessing and managing health and safety risks associated with ISEA’s premises, events, travel and operations;
  • consulting with employees, volunteers and other stakeholders on matters affecting their health, safety and wellbeing;
  • providing information, instruction, training and supervision necessary to enable people to carry out ISEA-related activities safely;
  • promoting positive psychological health and wellbeing, and providing appropriate support to those experiencing work-related stress or other health and wellbeing difficulties;
  • investigating incidents and near misses, and taking corrective action to prevent recurrence;
  • complying with applicable health and safety law in each jurisdiction in which ISEA operates; and
  • continually improving its health, safety and wellbeing performance over time.

This commitment applies equally to ISEA’s treatment of its employees and to the safety of volunteers, contractors, Members and other participants in ISEA activities, recognising that ISEA’s duty of care extends beyond its direct workforce to all those who engage with ISEA’s offices, events and operations.

Governance and Oversight

ISEA shall maintain a Health, Safety and Wellbeing (HSW) Committee, reporting to the Governing Council, responsible for overseeing the implementation, monitoring and continuous improvement of this Policy. Until decided otherwise by the Governing Council, the HSW Committee shall be the Executive Secretariat.

The HSW Committee’s responsibilities may include:

  • advising the Governing Council on ISEA’s health, safety and wellbeing strategy, priorities and resourcing;
  • reviewing significant incidents, hazards and risk assessments arising from ISEA offices, events and operations;
  • monitoring compliance with this Policy and applicable health and safety law across ISEA’s offices and jurisdictions;
  • reviewing event and conference risk assessments for higher-risk activities;
  • recommending health, safety and wellbeing training, resources and initiatives; and
  • recommending amendments to this Policy in accordance with Section 28.

The HSW Committee shall work closely with the Executive Secretariat, which retains day-to-day operational responsibility for health and safety management, and shall liaise, where relevant, with the Ethics & Professional Standards Committee, the EDI Committee and the Risk Management function on matters of overlap.

Responsibilities

Maintaining a safe and healthy environment is a shared responsibility across ISEA’s governance, staff, membership, volunteers and contractors.

6.1 Governing Council

The Governing Council is ultimately responsible for approving this Policy, ensuring adequate resourcing for health, safety and wellbeing management, and holding the Executive Secretariat accountable for implementation.

6.2 Secretary-General and Executive Secretariat

The Secretary-General, supported by the Executive Secretariat, is responsible for the day-to-day implementation of this Policy, including maintaining safe offices and systems of work, conducting and reviewing risk assessments, coordinating event and travel safety arrangements, managing incident reporting and investigation, and reporting to the Governing Council and the HSW Committee on health, safety and wellbeing performance.

6.3 Managers and Supervisors

Managers and supervisors are responsible for implementing this Policy within their teams and areas of responsibility, including identifying and addressing hazards, ensuring that employees and contractors under their direction receive necessary training, monitoring workload and wellbeing, and responding appropriately to concerns raised by their teams.

6.4 Employees

Employees are responsible for taking reasonable care of their own health and safety and that of others affected by their acts or omissions, complying with this Policy and related safety instructions, using equipment and facilities properly, reporting hazards and incidents promptly, and cooperating with ISEA’s health and safety measures.

6.5 Volunteers

Volunteers acting on behalf of ISEA, including examiners, assessors, mentors, speakers and chapter volunteers, are responsible for following applicable safety instructions and venue rules, reporting hazards and incidents of which they become aware, and taking reasonable care for their own safety and that of others while engaged in ISEA-related activities.

6.6 Contractors and Suppliers

Contractors, suppliers and venue operators engaged by ISEA are responsible for complying with applicable health and safety law and with any health and safety requirements specified in their engagement, for maintaining safe equipment, premises and systems of work under their control, and for cooperating with ISEA’s health and safety requirements and incident reporting processes.

6.7 Members and Event Attendees

Members and other attendees at ISEA events are expected to comply with venue safety rules and instructions given by ISEA staff, volunteers or venue personnel, to conduct themselves in a manner that does not endanger themselves or others, and to report hazards, incidents or safety concerns to ISEA staff or event organisers.

Office and Workplace Safety

ISEA is committed to maintaining offices and workplaces that are safe, well-maintained and appropriately equipped.

7.1 Workplace Environment

ISEA offices shall be maintained in a clean, orderly condition, with adequate lighting, ventilation, temperature control, and safe means of access and egress. Walkways and fire exits shall be kept clear at all times. Facilities and equipment shall be inspected and maintained in accordance with applicable local requirements and manufacturer guidance.

7.2 Ergonomics and Display Screen Equipment

ISEA will provide employees with workstations, seating and equipment that support good ergonomic practice, and will provide guidance on the safe and comfortable use of display screen equipment, including the importance of appropriate posture, screen positioning and regular breaks. Employees who require an ergonomic assessment or adjustment because of a health condition or disability may request one in accordance with ISEA’s Reasonable Accommodation processes under the Equality, Diversity and Inclusion Policy.

7.3 Fire Safety and Emergency Evacuation

Each ISEA office shall maintain a fire safety plan appropriate to the premises and applicable local law, including clearly marked fire exits and assembly points, working fire detection and, where required, suppression equipment, and periodic fire drills. Office managers are responsible for ensuring that new employees, contractors and visitors are made aware of evacuation procedures.

7.4 Building Security and Access Control

ISEA offices shall maintain appropriate access control and visitor management arrangements, having regard to the nature of the premises and the surrounding environment, to protect the safety of employees, contractors and visitors and the security of ISEA’s premises, records and assets.

7.5 Housekeeping, Equipment and Manual Handling

Employees and contractors shall be provided with guidance on safe manual handling and the safe use of office equipment. Spills, obstructions and other hazards should be reported and addressed promptly. Electrical equipment shall be maintained and inspected in accordance with applicable local requirements.

7.6 Lone Working and Personal Safety

Where an employee, volunteer or contractor is required to work alone at an ISEA office, or otherwise in circumstances of Lone Working, such as opening or closing an office outside normal hours, ISEA will take reasonable steps to assess and manage any associated risk, which may include providing a means of summoning assistance, establishing a check-in arrangement with a colleague or manager, and providing guidance on personal safety appropriate to the location. Employees and volunteers should raise any personal safety concern connected to Lone Working with their manager or the Executive Secretariat.

7.7 Workplace Inspections

ISEA offices shall be subject to periodic health and safety inspection, proportionate to the size and nature of the premises, in order to identify and address hazards before they result in harm. Inspection findings and any resulting corrective actions shall be recorded and tracked to completion.

Remote and Hybrid Working

ISEA recognises that many employees work remotely or on a hybrid basis, and that ISEA’s duty of care extends, so far as is reasonably practicable, to the health and safety of employees working from home or another remote location.

In relation to remote and hybrid working, ISEA will:

  • provide guidance on setting up a safe and ergonomically appropriate home working environment;
  • provide, where reasonably practicable, appropriate equipment to support safe remote working, such as suitable seating or display screen equipment;
  • encourage regular breaks and reasonable boundaries between working and non-working time; and
  • maintain channels through which remote employees can report hazards, incidents, or health and safety or wellbeing concerns, consistent with those available to office-based employees.

Employees working remotely remain responsible for maintaining a reasonably safe working environment and for promptly reporting any home-working health or safety concern to their manager or the Executive Secretariat.

Event and Conference Safety

ISEA organises and sanctions conferences, examinations, chapter meetings and other events across many jurisdictions, and recognises that these activities carry health and safety considerations distinct from ISEA’s offices.

9.1 Event Risk Assessment

A risk assessment shall be conducted for each significant ISEA-organised event, having regard to factors such as the size and nature of the event, the venue, the profile of attendees, and any specific hazards associated with the format of the event. The risk assessment shall be reviewed and updated where circumstances change materially before the event takes place.

9.2 Venue Selection

Venues for ISEA-organised events shall be selected having regard to their compliance with applicable local safety and building requirements, the adequacy of their fire safety and emergency arrangements, and their physical accessibility, consistent with Section 11.2 of the Equality, Diversity and Inclusion Policy.

9.3 Crowd Management and Capacity

Event organisers shall have regard to venue capacity limits, crowd management arrangements and safe means of entry and egress, particularly for larger conferences and examinations. Attendee numbers shall not be permitted to exceed safe or legally permitted venue capacity.

9.4 Emergency Planning at Events

Event organisers shall ensure that appropriate emergency arrangements are in place for each event, including a means of raising the alarm, clearly communicated evacuation procedures, accessible first aid provision consistent with Section 16, and a nominated person responsible for coordinating the response to an incident or emergency at the event.

9.5 Virtual and Hybrid Events

Where ISEA organises virtual or hybrid events, ISEA will have regard to the digital wellbeing of presenters and attendees, including reasonable scheduling across time zones, and will maintain a means by which participants can report technical or conduct-related concerns arising during the event.

9.6 Speaker, Presenter and Staff Wellbeing

ISEA recognises that employees, volunteers and speakers supporting the delivery of a conference or examination may experience long or irregular working hours around the event. Event organisers should have regard to reasonable rest breaks, adequate staffing levels, and access to food, water and rest facilities for ISEA staff and volunteers working at the event, consistent with the fatigue management principles in Section 13.5.

Business Travel Health and Safety

ISEA recognises that employees, Governing Council members, committee members and, on occasion, volunteers may be required to travel internationally in connection with ISEA business, and that international travel carries health and safety considerations distinct from office-based work.

10.1 Travel Risk Assessment

Before undertaking ISEA-related travel to a higher-risk destination, travellers and the Executive Secretariat shall have regard to relevant government travel advisories, health requirements such as recommended vaccinations, and any specific security or safety considerations applicable to the destination.

10.2 Duty of Care While Travelling

ISEA will take reasonable steps to support the safety of travellers undertaking ISEA business, including maintaining emergency contact arrangements, providing relevant travel and destination information, and, where reasonably practicable and proportionate to the risk profile of the destination, arranging appropriate travel insurance and emergency assistance cover.

10.3 Medical Emergencies and Evacuation

Travellers experiencing a medical emergency while on ISEA business should seek appropriate local medical assistance and notify the Executive Secretariat as soon as reasonably practicable. ISEA will provide reasonable support and assistance, including facilitating access to emergency medical evacuation or repatriation services, where such services are available under applicable travel insurance or emergency assistance arrangements.

Volunteer Health and Safety

ISEA relies significantly on the contribution of volunteers, including examiners, assessors, mentors, chapter leaders and event volunteers, and recognises a duty of care towards them while they are engaged in ISEA-related activities.

In relation to volunteers, ISEA will:

  • provide volunteers with relevant safety information and instruction appropriate to their role and the activity concerned;
  • ensure that volunteer roles involving higher-risk activities, if any, are subject to an appropriate risk assessment;
  • provide volunteers with access to the incident and hazard reporting channels described in Section 17; and
  • take reasonable steps to ensure that volunteers are not exposed to a materially greater risk than employees performing comparable activities.

Volunteers are encouraged to raise any health and safety concern regarding their role or a specific activity with the relevant committee chair, event organiser or the Executive Secretariat.

Contractor, Supplier and Venue Management

Where ISEA engages contractors, suppliers or venues to deliver services or premises in connection with ISEA activities, ISEA will take reasonable steps, proportionate to the nature and risk profile of the engagement, to:

  • assess the health and safety competence and track record of significant contractors, suppliers and venues before engagement, where reasonably practicable;
  • include appropriate health and safety expectations and, where relevant, insurance requirements, in contracts and event agreements;
  • coordinate health and safety responsibilities where ISEA staff, volunteers or attendees work alongside contractor or venue personnel; and
  • require contractors and venues to report to ISEA any incident affecting ISEA employees, volunteers, Members or attendees arising from their premises, equipment or services.

This Policy does not itself govern the internal health and safety management systems of independent contractor or venue organisations, which remain responsible for compliance with applicable law, but ISEA will take reasonable and proportionate steps, within its contractual relationships, to promote consistency with the principles of this Policy.

Mental Health and Psychological Wellbeing

ISEA recognises that psychological health and wellbeing are as important as physical health and safety, and is committed to fostering a working environment that supports positive mental health.

13.1 Promoting Positive Mental Health

ISEA will promote awareness of mental health and wellbeing among employees, volunteers and Members, encourage open conversation about mental health without stigma, and provide guidance and resources to support employees experiencing mental health difficulties, consistent with applicable law and the confidentiality principles in Section 23.

13.2 Work-Related Stress and Workload Management

ISEA will take reasonable steps to identify and manage factors that may contribute to excessive or harmful work-related stress, including unsustainable workload, unclear role expectations, and insufficient support or resources. Managers are expected to monitor workload within their teams and to raise concerns with the Executive Secretariat where sustained excessive demands are identified. Employees are encouraged to raise concerns about workload or work-related stress with their manager or through the channels described in Section 17.

13.3 Support Resources

Where reasonably practicable, ISEA will make available or signpost confidential support resources, such as an employee assistance programme, counselling services, or relevant external mental health resources, to employees and, where appropriate, to volunteers and Members experiencing mental health or wellbeing difficulties connected to their engagement with ISEA.

13.4 Right to Disconnect

ISEA supports reasonable boundaries between working and non-working time. Except where genuinely urgent or where an employee’s role requires otherwise, employees are not expected to routinely respond to work communications outside their normal working hours, having regard to time zone differences inherent in a global organisation.

13.5 Fatigue and Working Hours

ISEA recognises that fatigue arising from excessive working hours, insufficient rest, or extended international travel can impair judgement, increase the risk of error or incident, and adversely affect health. Managers should have regard to the cumulative effect of travel, event work and normal duties when scheduling employees and volunteers, and should take reasonable steps to ensure adequate rest periods, particularly around demanding events such as examinations and major conferences.

13.6 Support for New and Expectant Parents

ISEA will give reasonable consideration to the health and safety of employees who are pregnant, have recently given birth, or are returning to work after parental leave, including through appropriate risk assessment of their role and reasonable adjustments to duties, travel expectations or working arrangements where necessary, consistent with applicable local law.

Prevention of Workplace Violence, Aggression and Harassment

ISEA does not tolerate violence, threats, intimidation or aggressive conduct in its offices, at its events, or in connection with its activities. Conduct of this nature is addressed as a health and safety matter under this Policy and, where it involves harassment, bullying or victimisation related to a Protected Characteristic or otherwise, is addressed under ISEA’s Equality, Diversity and Inclusion Policy.

Where a person reasonably believes that they, or another person, are at risk of imminent harm, they should remove themselves from danger where safe to do so, seek assistance from venue security or local emergency services, and report the matter to ISEA as soon as reasonably practicable through the channels described in Section 17.

ISEA will take reasonable steps to assess and manage the risk of violence or aggression arising from identifiable circumstances, such as a contentious disciplinary matter, a distressed Applicant, or a known individual risk, and to protect employees and volunteers who may be exposed to such circumstances in the course of their role.

Alcohol, Substance Use and Event Safety

Alcohol may be served at certain ISEA social events and conference functions, consistent with applicable local law and professional norms. Where alcohol is served, ISEA expects attendees to consume it responsibly and expects hosts and event organisers to ensure that reasonable measures are in place, such as the availability of food and non-alcoholic beverages, and appropriate service standards consistent with venue and legal requirements.

The use of illegal drugs, or the misuse of alcohol or legal substances in a manner that impairs a person’s ability to safely perform their role or that endangers themselves or others, is not permitted in connection with any ISEA activity. Employees, volunteers and other individuals attending ISEA offices, examinations or events are expected not to attend, or to perform their role, while impaired by alcohol or a substance to an extent that compromises safety, professional conduct, or the integrity of an examination.

Concerns regarding a colleague’s or attendee’s apparent impairment should be raised promptly and sensitively with an ISEA staff member, event organiser or manager, having regard both to safety and to the wellbeing of the individual concerned.

First Aid and Medical Emergencies

ISEA will take reasonable steps to ensure that appropriate first aid provision is available at its offices and at significant ISEA-organised events, having regard to the size of the office or event, applicable local requirements, and the nature of the activities concerned.

First aid arrangements may include the appointment of trained first aiders among employees or event staff, the maintenance of appropriately stocked first aid kits, and clear signage or communication identifying first aid contacts and, where applicable, the location of automated external defibrillators.

In the event of a medical emergency, individuals should seek immediate assistance from local emergency services where appropriate, notify available first aiders or venue medical staff, and report the incident in accordance with Section 17 as soon as reasonably practicable thereafter.

Hazard and Incident Reporting

ISEA encourages the prompt reporting of hazards, incidents and near misses, in order to enable timely corrective action and to support continuous improvement in health, safety and wellbeing performance.

17.1 Who Should Report

Employees, volunteers, contractors, Members and event attendees are all encouraged to report hazards, incidents and near misses of which they become aware, whether or not they were personally affected.

17.2 How to Report

Reports may be made to a manager, event organiser, office administrator, the Executive Secretariat, or another designated health and safety contact identified at the relevant office or event. Serious incidents should be reported as soon as reasonably practicable and, in any event, before the person reporting leaves the office or event where safe and practicable to do so.

17.3 No Detriment for Good-Faith Reporting

ISEA will not subject any person to detriment for reporting, in good faith, a hazard, incident or health and safety concern under this Policy. This protection operates alongside, and is reinforced by, the protections available under ISEA’s Whistleblower Policy.

Incident Investigation and Corrective Action

ISEA will investigate reported incidents and near misses to a degree proportionate to their actual or potential severity, in order to understand the contributing causes and to identify appropriate corrective action.

ISEA’s incident investigation process is intended to:

  • establish the relevant facts and circumstances of the incident;
  • identify any immediate and underlying causes, including any failure of equipment, process or supervision;
  • identify corrective and preventive action to reduce the likelihood of recurrence; and
  • be conducted without attributing blame to any individual who reports an incident in good faith, consistent with Section 17.3.

Serious incidents, including those resulting in significant injury or requiring notification to a regulatory authority under applicable local law, shall be reported to the HSW Committee and, where appropriate, to the Governing Council.

Emergency Preparedness and Business Continuity

ISEA will maintain reasonable emergency preparedness arrangements for its offices and significant events, including emergency contact information, evacuation procedures, and, where appropriate to the size and risk profile of the office or activity, business continuity arrangements to maintain critical ISEA functions in the event of a significant disruption, such as a natural disaster, public health emergency, or significant security incident.

This Policy operates alongside ISEA’s Risk Management Policy in relation to broader organisational risk and business continuity planning, and the HSW Committee shall coordinate with the risk management function on matters of overlap.

Pandemic, Infectious Illness and Public Health Preparedness

Given the international nature of ISEA’s offices, conferences and examinations, ISEA recognises the importance of preparedness for pandemics, epidemics and other significant public health events that may affect its people, offices or events.

In relation to public health preparedness, ISEA will:

  • monitor relevant public health guidance issued by recognised national and international health authorities in the jurisdictions in which ISEA operates;
  • maintain the ability to adjust office working arrangements, such as remote working, in response to a significant public health event;
  • consider public health conditions as part of event risk assessments under Section 9.1, including, where appropriate, enhanced hygiene measures, capacity adjustments, or a decision to postpone, relocate or convert an event to a virtual or hybrid format; and
  • communicate clearly and promptly with employees, volunteers and Members regarding any public health measures affecting ISEA offices or events.

Employees, volunteers and attendees experiencing symptoms of a contagious illness are encouraged not to attend an ISEA office or event while contagious, consistent with any specific guidance issued for the relevant office or event, and consistent with applicable local law and any separate ISEA leave policies.

Occupational Health and Risk Assessment

ISEA will conduct risk assessments appropriate to its offices, events and operations, in order to identify hazards, evaluate risk, and determine appropriate control measures. Risk assessments shall be reviewed periodically and following any significant incident, change in premises, or material change in the nature of an activity.

21.1 Health Considerations for Specific Roles

Where an employee’s health condition may affect, or be affected by, their role, ISEA will, where reasonably practicable and with the employee’s consent, seek appropriate occupational health advice to inform any necessary workplace adjustments, consistent with the Reasonable Accommodation principles in ISEA’s Equality, Diversity and Inclusion Policy.

21.2 Fitness for Higher-Risk Activities

Where a specific ISEA-related activity carries a materially higher physical demand or risk, such as extended international travel to a demanding destination, ISEA may, where proportionate and consistent with applicable law, discuss Fitness for Role considerations with the individual concerned in order to identify any necessary support or adjustment, without requiring disclosure of a diagnosis or treating any disclosed health information other than in accordance with Section 23.

Training and Awareness

ISEA will provide health, safety and wellbeing training and information appropriate to the roles and activities of those to whom this Policy applies, including:

  • induction training for new employees, covering office safety, emergency procedures and incident reporting;
  • role-specific training for employees or volunteers undertaking higher-risk activities, such as event management or international travel;
  • periodic refresher training and awareness communications on topics such as mental health, ergonomics and emergency procedures; and
  • guidance materials for event organisers, chapter leaders and volunteers on event and travel safety expectations.

Managers are responsible for ensuring that employees and contractors within their area of responsibility complete health and safety training relevant to their role.

Health, Safety and Wellbeing Records and Privacy

Information relating to incidents, hazards, risk assessments and health and safety or wellbeing concerns will be recorded and retained in accordance with ISEA’s Document Retention Policy, and will be handled with appropriate confidentiality, disclosed only to those persons who reasonably need to know it in order to investigate and address the matter, ensure compliance with applicable law, or fulfil ISEA’s duty of care.

Personal information, including health-related information, collected in connection with this Policy will be processed in accordance with ISEA’s Privacy Policy and applicable data protection law, and will be used only for the purposes of managing health, safety and wellbeing, unless the individual concerned consents to a further use or disclosure is otherwise required by law.

Monitoring and Performance Review

ISEA will monitor its health, safety and wellbeing performance, including through:

  • tracking the number, nature and severity of reported incidents and near misses across offices and events;
  • periodic office and event safety inspections or audits, proportionate to the size and risk profile of the premises or activity;
  • periodic review of employee wellbeing indicators, such as sickness absence trends, where available and consistent with applicable privacy law; and
  • periodic reporting to the Governing Council on health, safety and wellbeing performance and initiatives.

Aggregated, non-identifying health, safety and wellbeing performance indicators may be referenced in ISEA’s annual or governance reporting, at the discretion of the Governing Council.

Non-Compliance and Consequences

Compliance with this Policy, and with reasonable safety instructions given by ISEA staff, event organisers or venue personnel, is a condition of participation in ISEA’s offices, events and activities.

Conduct that endangers the health or safety of any person, or that constitutes a serious or repeated breach of this Policy, may result in action proportionate to the nature and severity of the conduct, including:

  • a verbal or written reminder of applicable safety requirements;
  • removal from an ISEA office, event or examination;
  • for employees and contractors, disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment or engagement, in accordance with applicable local law and ISEA’s employment policies;
  • for Members, action under the Membership Regulations and Disciplinary Policy; and
  • referral to relevant emergency services, regulatory authorities or law enforcement, where appropriate.

Relationship with Other ISEA Policies

This Policy operates alongside, and should be interpreted consistently with, other ISEA policies, including:

  • the Equality, Diversity and Inclusion Policy, which addresses harassment, bullying, victimisation and Reasonable Accommodation;
  • the Ethics Policy and Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct, which establish ISEA’s broader ethical framework;
  • the Risk Management Policy, which addresses broader organisational risk, including business continuity;
  • the Whistleblower Policy, which establishes protected channels for reporting suspected wrongdoing and safeguards against retaliation;
  • the Complaints Policy and Disciplinary Policy, which establish the process for handling complaints and alleged breaches of ISEA requirements; and
  • the Privacy Policy and Document Retention Policy, which govern the handling and retention of personal information, including health-related information.

Where a matter is addressed by a specific procedure established by one of these policies, that procedure shall apply, together with the substantive standards established by this Policy.

Illustrative Examples

The following examples are provided to assist understanding of this Policy. They are illustrative only, are not exhaustive, and do not predetermine the outcome of any particular case, which will depend on its specific facts and circumstances.

27.1 Office Hazard

An employee notices a loose carpet tile creating a trip hazard near a fire exit. The employee should report the hazard to office management as soon as reasonably practicable in accordance with Section 17, so that it can be addressed before it causes injury.

27.2 Conference Emergency

At a large ISEA conference, an attendee experiences a medical emergency during a keynote session. Event staff should follow the event’s emergency plan under Section 9.4, summon first aid or local emergency services as appropriate, and the incident should be recorded and reported in accordance with Sections 17 and 18.

27.3 Work-Related Stress

An employee’s manager notices a sustained pattern of late-night emails and missed breaks over several weeks. Consistent with Section 13.2, the manager should proactively check in with the employee about workload and, where appropriate, involve the Executive Secretariat in reviewing the employee’s responsibilities.

27.4 International Travel Risk

An employee is asked to travel to a chapter event in a location subject to an elevated government travel advisory. Consistent with Section 10.1, the Executive Secretariat should review the advisory and relevant risk factors with the employee before confirming the trip, and consider appropriate mitigations or alternative arrangements, such as virtual participation.

Review and Continuous Improvement

ISEA shall periodically review this Policy to ensure that it remains appropriate, effective and consistent with developments in:

  • occupational health and safety law and good practice in the jurisdictions in which ISEA operates;
  • international standards and good practice in workplace health, safety and wellbeing management;
  • the nature, scale and locations of ISEA’s offices, events and operations; and
  • lessons learned from incidents, near misses and monitoring data collected under Sections 18 and 23.

The HSW Committee shall recommend amendments to this Policy where appropriate, subject to approval by the Governing Council.

Effective Date

This Workplace Health, Safety and Wellbeing Policy takes effect on the date approved by the Governing Council.

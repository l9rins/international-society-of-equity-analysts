Governance Policy

Intellectual Property Policy

International Society of Equity Analysts

Version: 1.2 Approved: 1 July 2025 Last Updated: 1 July 2026 Administered by: AI Technology and Innovation Committee
This document forms part of the official governance framework of the International Society of Equity Analysts (ISEA) and should be read together with the related documents listed above.

Purpose

The International Society of Equity Analysts (“ISEA”) creates, publishes, commissions, receives and relies upon a wide range of intellectual property in the course of pursuing its mission to promote professionalism, competence and ethical conduct within the global equity research and investment analysis profession.

This intellectual property includes, without limitation, research reports, publications, educational and course materials, continuing professional development (“CPD”) materials, trademarks, logos, professional designations, website content, member submissions and conference presentations.

This Intellectual Property Policy (“Policy”) establishes a clear and consistent framework governing the ownership, protection, licensing and permitted use of intellectual property created, published, submitted to, or administered by ISEA. It is intended to:

  • protect the value, integrity and reputation of ISEA’s intellectual property, including its name, trademarks, logos and professional designations;
  • provide clarity to Members, employees, officers, Governing Council members, committee members, contractors, volunteers, applicants and other stakeholders regarding the ownership of, and rights in, materials created or submitted in connection with ISEA’s activities;
  • establish a consistent basis upon which ISEA may use, reproduce, publish, archive and otherwise deal with content submitted to it, including research reports submitted as part of the Equity Research Assessment component of the MISEA (Member of the International Society of Equity Analysts) application process;
  • safeguard the integrity of the MISEA and FISEA (Fellow of the International Society of Equity Analysts) designations and other ISEA certifications;
  • respect the intellectual property rights of third parties, including Members, contributors, speakers, applicants and licensors; and
  • support ISEA’s continued operation as a professional association with an international membership base and a global reputation to protect.

This Policy operates alongside, and should be read together with, the Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct (“Code”) and other applicable ISEA policies, regulations and governance requirements.

Scope and Application

This Policy applies to:

  • Governing Council members;
  • officers of ISEA;
  • committee members;
  • employees and personnel of the Executive Secretariat;
  • ISEA Members (Student Members, Associate Members, Members and Fellows);
  • applicants and candidates for ISEA membership, certification or designations, including participants in the Equity Research Assessment;
  • holders of ISEA professional certifications and designations;
  • contractors, consultants, examiners, assessors, moderators, education providers and other service providers engaged by ISEA;
  • volunteers and representatives acting on behalf of ISEA;
  • speakers, presenters and contributors to ISEA conferences, seminars, webinars and publications; and
  • users of the ISEA website and other ISEA digital platforms,
  • (each, a “Relevant Person”, and collectively, “Relevant Persons”).

This Policy applies to all forms of intellectual property relevant to ISEA’s activities, including copyright works, trademarks, service marks, trade names, logos, designs, database rights, domain names and confidential information to the extent relevant to intellectual property matters, whether or not formally registered, and irrespective of the jurisdiction in which such rights subsist or are enforceable.

By becoming a Member of ISEA, submitting an application for membership or a professional designation, obtaining an ISEA certification or designation, holding an ISEA governance or representative position, contributing content to ISEA, or otherwise participating in an ISEA program or activity, an individual acknowledges and agrees to be bound by this Policy.

Where this Policy conflicts with a specific provision of another ISEA policy, regulation or a written agreement to which ISEA is a party (such as a licensing, employment, engagement or sponsorship agreement), the more specific provision shall prevail to the extent of the inconsistency, unless otherwise stated.

Definitions

For the purposes of this Policy, the following terms have the meanings set out below.

Intellectual Property: any and all rights subsisting in copyright works, trademarks, service marks, trade names, logos, designs, database rights, know-how, domain names and similar rights, whether registered or unregistered, and wherever in the world such rights may subsist.

ISEA Intellectual Property: Intellectual Property owned by, or licensed to, ISEA, including the ISEA name, the ISEA logo, the MISEA and FISEA designations, ISEA publications, educational and Course Materials, CPD Materials, website content and any other materials created, commissioned or published by ISEA.

Course Materials: curricula, syllabi, study guides, question banks, e-learning modules, presentation slides, instructional videos, case studies and similar materials developed or approved by ISEA for use in its education, examination or certification programs.

Course Materials: curricula, syllabi, textbooks, study guides, question banks, examination-preparation materials, e-learning modules, presentation slides, instructional videos, case studies, research materials, manuals and similar materials developed, commissioned, licensed, published or otherwise supplied or approved by ISEA for use in its education, examination, assessment or certification programs, including materials supplied to applicants or candidates participating in the Certified Equity Analyst Program.

CPD Materials: webinars, articles, workshop content, accreditation criteria and similar materials created for the purposes of ISEA’s Continuing Professional Development program, whether developed internally by ISEA or by an approved CPD provider.

Equity Research Assessment: the component of the MISEA application process, described in the Member Admission Framework, under which each applicant is required to submit two original equity research reports for assessment.

Submitted Research Reports: the two equity research reports submitted by an applicant as part of the Equity Research Assessment.

Member Submission: any article, case study, comment, forum or community post, testimonial, photograph, video or other content voluntarily submitted by a Member or other Relevant Person to ISEA, other than a Submitted Research Report or Course Material, addressed in Section 12.

Publications: journals, whitepapers, research bulletins, newsletters, books, standards, best-practice guides and similar works authored, commissioned or published by ISEA.

Designations: the MISEA and FISEA post-nominal designations and any other professional certification or designation conferred by ISEA, together with any associated certificates, digital badges or marks.

Licence: a permission granted by the owner of Intellectual Property authorising another person to exercise specified rights in that Intellectual Property, which may be exclusive or non-exclusive, and which does not transfer ownership.

Moral Rights: the rights of an author recognised in certain jurisdictions to be identified as the author of a work and to object to derogatory treatment of that work, to the extent such rights are capable of being waived or consented to under applicable law.

Data and Databases: structured collections of data compiled, maintained or licensed by ISEA, including the member directory, examination and assessment records, CPD records and website analytics, together with any database rights subsisting in such collections.

Domain Names and Social Media Accounts: internet domain names, social media handles, usernames and similar online identifiers registered or used by ISEA to identify itself or its programs.

Other capitalised terms used in this Policy have the meanings given to them in the Membership Regulations, the Member Admission Framework, the Code, or elsewhere in this Policy.

General Principles of Ownership of Intellectual Property

4.1 Intellectual Property Created in the Course of ISEA Duties

Except as otherwise agreed in writing, all Intellectual Property created by Governing Council members, officers, committee members, employees of the Executive Secretariat, contractors, consultants and volunteers in the course of performing their duties, functions or engagement for ISEA vests in, and is owned exclusively by, ISEA. This includes Intellectual Property created using ISEA resources, on ISEA time, or in connection with an ISEA role, program or activity.

4.2 Intellectual Property Created Independently of ISEA

Intellectual Property created by a Member or other Relevant Person independently of their ISEA role, and not created for, commissioned by, or submitted to ISEA, remains the property of that individual (or their employer, where applicable) and is not affected by this Policy.

4.3 Commissioned Works

Where ISEA commissions an individual or organisation to create Intellectual Property (including authors, speakers, designers, developers or education providers), ownership shall be governed by the terms of the applicable written engagement or licensing agreement. In the absence of a written agreement providing otherwise, ISEA shall be treated as having obtained, at minimum, a perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free licence to use the commissioned work for the purposes for which it was commissioned.

4.4 Jointly Created Intellectual Property

Where Intellectual Property is created jointly by ISEA and one or more other parties, ownership and usage rights shall be governed by a written agreement between the parties. In the absence of such an agreement, ISEA shall be entitled to a non-exclusive, perpetual, royalty-free licence to use the jointly created Intellectual Property for its professional, educational and administrative purposes.

ISEA Trademarks and Logos

5.1 Ownership

The name “International Society of Equity Analysts”, the acronym “ISEA”, the ISEA logo, and any other distinctive names, marks, taglines or visual identity elements adopted by ISEA from time to time (collectively, the “ISEA Marks”) are the exclusive property of ISEA, whether or not formally registered as trademarks in a particular jurisdiction.

5.2 Protection

ISEA may register the ISEA Marks as trademarks or other protected designations in jurisdictions where it operates or has a legitimate commercial or reputational interest, and may take such steps as it considers appropriate to protect and enforce its rights in the ISEA Marks, including registration, monitoring, cease-and-desist action and legal proceedings.

5.3 Permitted Use by Members

Subject to the Membership Regulations and any applicable brand guidelines issued by ISEA, a Member in good standing may:

  • refer to their membership category and, where applicable, their MISEA or FISEA designation, in accordance with Section 6;
  • use an ISEA-issued digital member badge or certificate mark in a manner consistent with any usage guidelines accompanying that badge; and
  • truthfully describe their affiliation with ISEA (for example, “Member of the International Society of Equity Analysts”) in professional biographies, correspondence and marketing materials.

5.4 Prohibited Use

No Relevant Person may, without ISEA’s prior written consent:

  • use the ISEA Marks in a manner that suggests ISEA endorsement, sponsorship or approval of any product, service, opinion or organisation;
  • modify, distort, recolour or combine the ISEA logo with other marks or imagery;
  • use the ISEA Marks, or any confusingly similar mark, as or within a domain name, company name, trading name, social media handle or similar identifier;
  • continue to use the ISEA Marks, a Designation, or any associated badge or certificate after the individual’s membership, certification or Designation has lapsed, been suspended, or been revoked; or
  • otherwise use the ISEA Marks in a manner that is misleading, unlawful or damaging to ISEA’s reputation.

5.5 Brand Guidelines

ISEA may issue and update, from time to time, brand and style guidelines governing the presentation and use of the ISEA Marks. Relevant Persons authorised to use the ISEA Marks shall comply with such guidelines as in force from time to time.

5.6 Domain Names and Social Media Accounts

Internet domain names, social media handles and usernames that incorporate an ISEA Mark, or that are used to represent an ISEA committee, chapter, program or event, are ISEA Intellectual Property. Such domain names and accounts shall be registered in ISEA’s name, or in the name of a person expressly authorised to hold them on ISEA’s behalf, and access credentials shall be provided to the Executive Secretariat.

A local chapter, interest group or committee that wishes to establish a dedicated website or social media presence using an ISEA Mark must first obtain the Executive Secretariat’s written approval and shall transfer control of the relevant account to ISEA upon request or upon the chapter, group or committee ceasing to operate.

Professional Designations (MISEA and FISEA)

6.1 Nature of the Designations

The MISEA and FISEA designations, the associated post-nominal letters, and any digital badge, certificate or seal issued in connection with them, are proprietary marks of ISEA. Conferral of a Designation recognises that an individual has met the applicable eligibility, assessment and other requirements set out in the Membership Policy, Membership Regulations and Member Admission Framework.

6.2 Licence to Use

Conferral of a Designation grants the recipient a personal, non-exclusive, non-transferable and revocable licence to use the applicable post-nominal letters, and any associated badge or certificate mark, for so long as the individual remains a Member in good standing and continues to satisfy the applicable requirements of the Membership Policy, Membership Regulations, CPD Policy and the Code. This licence does not transfer any ownership interest in the Designation to the individual.

6.3 Suspension, Lapse and Revocation

Where an individual’s membership or Designation is suspended, lapses or is revoked in accordance with the Membership Policy, Membership Regulations or Disciplinary Policy, the individual’s licence to use the Designation, and any associated post-nominal letters, badge or certificate mark, is automatically suspended or revoked accordingly.

The individual must promptly cease using the Designation and any associated marks, and must remove references to the Designation from business cards, letterhead, email signatures, websites, social media profiles and other materials within thirty (30) days of suspension or revocation, except where the applicable ISEA process expressly permits continued reference to a former or lapsed status (for example, “Former FISEA”, where permitted).

6.4 No Proprietary Interest

A Member does not acquire any ownership, equitable or other proprietary interest in a Designation, or in the ISEA Marks associated with it, by virtue of holding that Designation.

6.5 Register of Designation Holders

ISEA may maintain, and make available to the public, a register or directory confirming whether a named individual currently holds a Designation and is in good standing. Maintenance of the register does not create any additional Intellectual Property right in favour of an individual Member, and ISEA may rely on the register to verify or refute a claimed Designation, including in connection with a suspected breach of Section 5.4 or Section 6.3.

Publications

7.1 Scope

This Section applies to journals, whitepapers, research bulletins, newsletters, books, standards, best-practice guides and other works authored, commissioned or published by ISEA (“Publications”).

7.2 Ownership

Publications are owned by ISEA, except where a Publication incorporates content contributed by an external author under a separate licensing or contribution agreement, in which case ownership and licensing of that content shall be governed by that agreement and, in the absence of specific terms, by Section 14 of this Policy. Where an external author contributes to a Publication, the author retains their Moral Rights in accordance with Section 17, but grants ISEA the rights described in Section 14 to publish, reproduce and distribute the work as part of the Publication.

7.3 Use by Members and the Public

Members and other readers may access and use Publications for their own personal professional reference and non-commercial purposes, including reasonable quotation with appropriate attribution to ISEA and, where applicable, the individual author. Publications, or substantial portions of them, may not be reproduced, republished, distributed, sold or otherwise commercially exploited, whether in whole or in part, without ISEA’s prior written permission.

Educational Materials and Course Materials

8.1 Scope

This Section applies to Course Materials developed or approved by ISEA for use in its Student Member, Associate Member, Member and Fellow education pathways, examinations and certification programs, including curricula, syllabi, study guides, question banks, e-learning modules, presentation slides, instructional videos and case studies.

8.2 Ownership

Course Materials are owned by ISEA or its licensors. Where Course Materials are developed by a third party under a commissioning or partnership arrangement, ownership shall be governed by the applicable written agreement and, in the absence of specific terms, by Section 4.3 of this Policy.

8.3 Certified Equity Analyst Program

This Section applies in particular to Course Materials supplied by ISEA to applicants and candidates participating in the Certified Equity Analyst Program, including materials supplied for the purpose of preparing for or completing any written examination, equity research assessment, preparation and defence of equity research reports, oral examination or other assessment component of the Program.

Course Materials supplied to an applicant or candidate for the Certified Equity Analyst Program remain the property of ISEA or the applicable third-party licensor. The applicant or candidate receives only the limited licence described in Section 8.2 and acquires no ownership or other proprietary interest in those materials by virtue of receiving, purchasing, accessing or using them.

8.4 Licence to Candidates and Permitted Use

Course Materials supplied by ISEA to an enrolled student, applicant or candidate are provided solely for the purpose of enabling that person to undertake the relevant ISEA education, examination, assessment or certification program. Except where expressly stated otherwise in writing, ownership of the Course Materials remains with ISEA or the applicable third-party licensor, and no ownership interest in the Course Materials is transferred to the student, applicant or candidate.

Subject to this Policy and any additional terms notified by ISEA, ISEA grants the relevant student, applicant or candidate a limited, personal, non-exclusive, non-transferable, non-sublicensable and revocable licence to access and use the Course Materials solely for their own personal study, preparation and participation in the relevant ISEA program.

Unless expressly authorised in writing by ISEA, a student, applicant or candidate must not:

  • sell, resell, rent, lease, lend, assign, transfer, sublicense or otherwise provide access to any Course Materials to another person;
  • copy, reproduce, scan, photograph, record, digitise or otherwise reproduce Course Materials, except to the extent reasonably necessary for the person's own permitted study and examination preparation or as expressly authorised by ISEA;
  • distribute, publish, transmit, upload, post, share or otherwise make Course Materials available, in whole or in part, to any other person or organisation;
  • upload or submit Course Materials to any third-party website, file-sharing service, artificial intelligence system, generative AI tool, database or other platform where doing so would make the materials available to, or accessible by, a third party;
  • use Course Materials to provide or develop commercial tutoring, coaching, training, examination-preparation or other educational services;
  • modify, adapt, translate, create derivative works from, or otherwise commercially exploit Course Materials; or
  • remove, obscure or alter any copyright notice, attribution, watermark, digital rights management measure or other proprietary notice contained in or accompanying the Course Materials.

The licence granted under this Section is limited to the period and purposes specified by ISEA and automatically terminates upon completion, withdrawal from, expiration of, or termination of the relevant program or candidacy, unless ISEA expressly permits continued access or use.

Upon termination of the licence, ISEA may require the student, applicant or candidate to cease using the Course Materials and, where reasonably practicable, return or permanently delete copies in their possession or control, subject to any copies that must be retained by law or that are automatically maintained in routine electronic backup systems.

Nothing in this Section prevents a student, applicant or candidate from exercising any right that cannot lawfully be excluded or restricted under applicable intellectual property law.

8.5 Approved Education Providers

ISEA may authorise approved or affiliated education and training providers to use specified Course Materials, or to develop supplementary materials aligned with ISEA’s curriculum, under a separate written licence or partnership agreement. Use of ISEA’s curriculum, Course Materials or ISEA Marks by an education provider that has not been so authorised is prohibited.

8.6 Use of Artificial Intelligence in Developing and Using Course Materials

Where artificial intelligence or other automated tools are used by ISEA, an approved education provider, or a contracted author in developing Course Materials, the individual or organisation responsible for that development remains accountable for the accuracy, originality and quality of the resulting content, consistently with the Ethics Policy. Ownership of Course Materials developed with the assistance of such tools is determined in accordance with Section 4 as though the tool were used by the relevant human author. A candidate or student who uses artificial intelligence or other automated tools in the course of their studies remains subject to the Code and any examination integrity requirements applicable to their program, and must not represent work substantially generated by such a tool as their own original analysis where the relevant assessment requires original work.

CPD Materials

9.1 Scope

This Section applies to CPD Materials developed for the purposes of ISEA’s Continuing Professional Development program, whether developed internally by ISEA or by an approved CPD provider, and is to be read together with the CPD Policy.

9.2 Ownership and Licensing

CPD Materials developed internally by ISEA are owned by ISEA. CPD Materials developed by an approved external CPD provider remain the property of that provider, who grants ISEA a licence, on terms agreed between the parties, to list, promote, accredit and, where agreed, host or distribute the relevant CPD Materials to Members for CPD purposes.

9.3 Accreditation Marks

Any accreditation mark, seal or statement (such as “ISEA-Accredited CPD”) used to denote that an activity or provider has been accredited by ISEA for CPD purposes is an ISEA Mark. An approved CPD provider may use such a mark only in relation to accredited activities, only while its accreditation remains current, and only in accordance with any conditions attached to that accreditation.

Website Content

10.1 Scope

This Section applies to the ISEA website, member portal, mobile applications and other digital platforms operated by or on behalf of ISEA, including their design, layout, graphics, software, source code, databases, member directories and written content (collectively, “Website Content”).

10.2 Ownership

Website Content is owned by ISEA or its licensors, including third-party developers or vendors who create Website Content under a commissioning arrangement, in which case Section 4.3 applies. Nothing in this Section affects the ownership of Member-generated content addressed in Section 12.

10.3 Use of the Website

Members and visitors may access and use the ISEA website and other digital platforms for ordinary browsing and personal reference purposes. Unless expressly authorised, no person may:

  • systematically extract, scrape, harvest or data-mine Website Content, including the member directory;
  • reproduce, republish, frame or mirror Website Content on another website or platform;
  • reverse-engineer, decompile or attempt to extract the source code underlying any ISEA digital platform; or
  • use any automated means to access, monitor or copy Website Content other than through a publicly available and appropriately authorised interface.

10.4 Data and Databases

Structured data compiled or maintained by ISEA, including the member directory, examination and assessment records, CPD records and website or platform analytics, together with any database rights subsisting in such compilations, are ISEA Intellectual Property. Access to such data by Members, employees, contractors and service providers is permitted only to the extent necessary for the performance of their role or the exercise of rights expressly granted to Members (such as reasonable use of the member directory for professional networking purposes consistent with the Privacy Policy), and is otherwise subject to the Privacy Policy and any applicable data-sharing or service agreement.

Research Reports Submitted as Part of the Equity Research Assessment

11.1 Overview

As described in the Member Admission Framework, each applicant for MISEA membership is required to submit two original equity research reports as part of the Equity Research Assessment component of their application. This Section governs the ownership and permitted use of Submitted Research Reports.

11.2 Ownership Retained by the Applicant

As between ISEA and the applicant, the applicant retains ownership of the copyright subsisting in their Submitted Research Reports. Submission of a research report as part of the Equity Research Assessment does not transfer ownership of that report to ISEA.

11.3 Licence Granted to ISEA

By submitting a research report as part of the Equity Research Assessment, an applicant grants ISEA a non-exclusive, perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide and royalty-free licence to:

  • reproduce, store, and internally distribute the Submitted Research Report for the purpose of assessing the application;
  • use the Submitted Research Report to train, calibrate and evaluate assessors, examiners and moderators involved in the Equity Research Assessment process;
  • use the Submitted Research Report for quality assurance, benchmarking, moderation and accreditation purposes, including retrospective review of assessment standards;
  • retain the Submitted Research Report as part of ISEA’s records in accordance with the Document Retention Policy and for the purpose of demonstrating the integrity of the Designation to regulators, accreditation bodies or in legal proceedings, where necessary; and
  • use the Submitted Research Report, or extracts from it, in a de-identified or anonymised form, as an illustrative or exemplar work in training materials for future assessors, moderators or candidates, provided that reasonable steps are taken to remove or obscure information that would identify the applicant.

ISEA will not publish, distribute externally, or otherwise make available a Submitted Research Report in a form that identifies the applicant, other than to persons directly involved in administering the Equity Research Assessment, without the applicant’s prior written consent, except where anonymised in accordance with this Section, or as required by Section 11.4 or by law.

11.4 Confidentiality

ISEA will treat Submitted Research Reports as confidential and will handle them in accordance with the Privacy Policy and this Policy. Submitted Research Reports may be disclosed to Governing Council members, committee members, employees, assessors, examiners and moderators on a need-to-know basis for the purpose of administering the Equity Research Assessment, and may otherwise be disclosed where required by law, where necessary to investigate a suspected breach of the Code or this Policy, or as otherwise permitted under this Policy.

11.5 Applicant Warranties

By submitting a research report as part of the Equity Research Assessment, an applicant warrants that:

  • the report is their own original work, prepared substantially by them for the purpose of the application, and does not infringe the copyright or other Intellectual Property rights of any third party, including a current or former employer;
  • where the report draws on data, models, templates or material belonging to a current or former employer or other third party, the applicant has all necessary rights, consents and authorisations to use and submit that material for the purposes of the Equity Research Assessment, and its inclusion does not breach any confidentiality, employment or contractual obligation owed by the applicant;
  • the report does not contain, and was not prepared using, material non-public information obtained in breach of law, regulation or a duty of confidentiality; and
  • the report complies with ISEA’s standards on plagiarism, originality and academic and professional integrity, as set out in the Code and the Member Admission Framework.

11.6 Consequences of Breach

Where ISEA determines that a Submitted Research Report breaches a warranty in Section 11.5, or otherwise involves plagiarism, fabrication or the improper use of confidential or material non-public information, ISEA may reject the application, decline to confer the Designation, or, where the Designation has already been conferred, refer the matter for investigation and possible action under the Disciplinary Policy, up to and including suspension or revocation of the Designation, in accordance with the Membership Regulations and the Code.

11.7 Retention and Disposal

Submitted Research Reports will be retained by ISEA in accordance with the Document Retention Policy, including for such period as is necessary to support the integrity and defensibility of the Designation and to comply with applicable legal or regulatory requirements. An applicant may request the return or deletion of their Submitted Research Reports following the applicable retention period, subject to ISEA’s legitimate recordkeeping, accreditation and legal obligations.

11.8 No Commercial Publication by ISEA

Except as set out in Section 11.3, ISEA will not commercially publish, sell, license to a third party for commercial purposes, or otherwise exploit a Submitted Research Report in a manner that identifies the applicant, without that applicant’s prior written consent.

11.9 Use of Artificial Intelligence Tools in Preparing Submitted Research Reports

An applicant remains personally responsible for the analysis, conclusions and professional judgement reflected in a Submitted Research Report, consistent with the Ethics Policy, regardless of whether artificial intelligence, automated data analytics or other technology tools were used in its preparation. An applicant who uses such tools must exercise appropriate oversight and verification of the output, must not represent analysis substantially generated by such a tool as their own independent professional judgement, and must disclose the use of such tools to the extent required by the Member Admission Framework or applicable assessment guidance. Use of such tools in a manner that is undisclosed where disclosure is required, or that results in a report that does not reflect the applicant’s own analytical work, may be treated as a breach of the warranties in Section 11.5.

Member Submissions

12.1 Scope

This Section applies to articles, case studies, comments, forum or community posts, testimonials, photographs, videos and other content voluntarily submitted by a Member or other Relevant Person to ISEA for publication or use by ISEA, other than Submitted Research Reports (Section 11), Course Materials (Section 8) or conference presentation materials (Section 13).

12.2 Ownership Retained by the Contributor

A contributor retains ownership of the copyright in their Member Submission, unless the contributor and ISEA agree otherwise in writing (for example, in a commissioned-article agreement).

12.3 License Granted to ISEA

By submitting a Member Submission, the contributor grants ISEA a non-exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free, and sub-licensable (to ISEA’s service providers as reasonably necessary for hosting, printing or distribution) licence to reproduce, publish, edit, format, translate, distribute and archive the Member Submission, in whole or in part, in any ISEA publication, website, digital platform, social media channel or other medium, with attribution to the contributor unless anonymity is requested by the contributor and agreed to by ISEA, or attribution is not otherwise appropriate in the circumstances.

12.4 Editorial Rights

ISEA may edit a Member Submission for style, length, clarity, tone and compliance with ISEA’s editorial standards and this Policy, provided that ISEA will not materially alter the substantive meaning of a Member Submission without the contributor’s consent, except where necessary to comply with law, avoid defamation or protect confidential or material non-public information.

12.5 Removal

ISEA may decline to publish, or may remove, a Member Submission that breaches the Code, the Privacy Policy, this Policy, or applicable law, or that ISEA otherwise reasonably considers inappropriate for publication.

12.6 Contributor Warranties

By submitting a Member Submission, the contributor warrants that it is original, does not infringe the Intellectual Property rights of any third party, does not breach any duty of confidentiality (including in relation to material non-public information), and complies with the Code.

Conference Presentations and Speaker Materials

13.1 Scope and Ownership

This Section applies to slide decks, speaker notes, panel materials and recordings prepared for or arising from ISEA conferences, seminars, webinars and similar events (“Conference Materials”). A speaker or presenter retains ownership of the copyright in the original content of their Conference Materials, subject to the licence granted under Section 13.2. ISEA owns the production elements of an event, including event recordings as produced and edited by or for ISEA, event branding, and any compiled event program.

13.2 Licence Granted to ISEA

By presenting at, or providing materials for, an ISEA event, a speaker grants ISEA a non-exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free licence to record, livestream, reproduce and distribute their Conference Materials and any recording of their presentation to event attendees, and, where agreed with the speaker, to the broader ISEA membership or CPD library, and to archive such materials for CPD accreditation, recordkeeping and promotional purposes.

13.3 Speaker Use of Recordings

A speaker may use a recording of their own presentation for their personal professional portfolio, with attribution to the relevant ISEA event, but may not sell, sublicense or otherwise commercially exploit the recording without ISEA’s prior written consent.

13.4 Third-Party Content in Conference Materials

A speaker is responsible for ensuring that they hold all necessary rights, consents and licences for any third-party material (including images, data, quotations or copyrighted works) incorporated into their Conference Materials, and for ensuring that such material is properly attributed.

Licence Granted to ISEA by Members, Contributors, Applicants and Speakers

14.1 General Nature of Licence

Except where a more specific provision of this Policy or a written agreement states otherwise, the licences granted to ISEA under Sections 11, 12 and 13 are non-exclusive, worldwide and royalty-free, and are sub-licensable to ISEA’s service providers to the extent reasonably necessary to enable ISEA to host, print, distribute or otherwise deal with the relevant material for the purposes described in those Sections.

14.2 Duration

Unless a narrower duration is specified for a particular category of material, a licence granted to ISEA under this Policy continues for the full duration of the applicable Intellectual Property rights, including after the contributor’s membership with ISEA ends, to the extent necessary to allow ISEA to continue to use material that has already been published, archived or incorporated into ISEA’s records in accordance with this Policy.

14.3 Moral Rights

To the extent permitted by applicable law, a contributor consents to ISEA making reasonable editorial, formatting and stylistic changes to their contribution, and to ISEA using their contribution consistently with the purposes described in this Policy, without such use constituting an infringement of the contributor’s Moral Rights. Section 17 sets out further provisions relating to Moral Rights.

14.4 Withdrawal

A contributor may request that ISEA cease future use of their Member Submission or Conference Materials by giving written notice to the Executive Secretariat. ISEA will use reasonable efforts to give effect to such a request for future use, but withdrawal of consent does not affect the validity of any use that occurred, or copies that were distributed or archived, before ISEA received the request, nor does it apply to Submitted Research Reports, which are governed exclusively by Section 11.

Use of Third-Party Intellectual Property by ISEA

15.1 ISEA’s Approach

ISEA respects the Intellectual Property rights of third parties. Where ISEA uses third-party material in its Publications, Course Materials, CPD Materials, website or other ISEA Intellectual Property, including stock images, licensed data, quoted material or licensed software, ISEA will seek to obtain appropriate rights, licences or permissions, and will attribute the material appropriately where required.

15.2 Obligations of Contributors

A Relevant Person who submits, incorporates or references third-party material in a Submitted Research Report, Member Submission, Conference Material, or other content provided to ISEA is responsible for ensuring that they hold the necessary rights, consents or licences to do so, and for ensuring that the material is properly attributed in accordance with applicable citation and referencing standards.

15.3 Notice and Takedown

Where ISEA becomes aware that material it hosts or publishes may infringe the Intellectual Property rights of a third party, ISEA will investigate the matter in accordance with Section 18 and take such action as it considers appropriate, which may include removing or disabling access to the material pending investigation.

Permitted and Prohibited Use of ISEA Intellectual Property

16.1 Permitted Use

Subject to the other provisions of this Policy, a Member in good standing may:

  • use their Designation and associated post-nominal letters in accordance with Section 6;
  • use an authorised ISEA member badge or certificate mark in accordance with any applicable usage guidelines;
  • access and use Publications, Course Materials and CPD Materials for personal, non-commercial professional reference and development; and
  • quote from ISEA Publications and other ISEA Intellectual Property with appropriate attribution, consistent with ordinary standards of fair citation.

16.2 Prohibited Use

Unless expressly authorised in writing by ISEA, no Relevant Person may:

  • commercially exploit, resell, sublicense or distribute ISEA Intellectual Property, including Course Materials, CPD Materials or Publications;
  • represent or imply that ISEA endorses, sponsors or is affiliated with any product, service, publication or organisation;
  • modify, adapt or create derivative works based on ISEA Intellectual Property, other than for permitted personal, non-commercial use;
  • use a Designation, ISEA Mark or associated badge after the relevant membership, certification or Designation has lapsed, been suspended or been revoked; or
  • use ISEA Intellectual Property in any manner that is unlawful, misleading, or that ISEA reasonably considers damaging to its reputation or that of its Members.

16.3 Use by Employers and Other Organisations

An employer or other organisation may accurately refer to an individual’s ISEA membership or Designation, for example in professional biographies or marketing materials, provided that the reference accurately reflects the individual’s current status and does not state or imply that ISEA endorses the organisation, its products or its services. Any other use of the ISEA Marks by an employer or other organisation requires ISEA’s prior written consent.

Moral Rights

To the extent recognised by applicable law, an author or contributor retains the Moral Rights in their original work, including the right to be identified as its author. ISEA will use reasonable efforts to attribute authorship appropriately when publishing or distributing a contributor’s work.

By submitting a Member Submission, Conference Material, Submitted Research Report, or other original content to ISEA, a contributor consents, to the extent permitted by applicable law, to ISEA making reasonable editorial, formatting and technical modifications to the work, using extracts of the work, and using the work in a de-identified or anonymised form in the circumstances described in this Policy, without such acts constituting an infringement of the contributor’s Moral Rights.

Infringement, Misuse and Unauthorised Use

18.1 Reporting

A Member, employee, contributor or member of the public who becomes aware of suspected infringement of ISEA Intellectual Property, or suspected misuse of a Designation or the ISEA Marks, may report the matter to the Executive Secretariat or such other contact as ISEA may designate from time to time.

18.2 Investigation

ISEA will assess and, where appropriate, investigate reports of suspected infringement or misuse. Where the matter involves a Member or other Relevant Person, the matter may be referred for consideration under the Complaints Policy or Disciplinary Policy, as applicable.

18.3 Remedial Action

Depending on the nature and severity of the conduct, ISEA may:

  • issue a request or notice requiring that the infringing or unauthorised use cease and any infringing material be removed;
  • require the return or destruction of unauthorised copies of ISEA Intellectual Property;
  • notify relevant online platforms, regulators or professional bodies of the unauthorised use;
  • refer the matter for disciplinary action in accordance with the Disciplinary Policy, where a Member or Designation holder is involved; and
  • pursue such civil, administrative or other legal remedies as ISEA considers appropriate.

Enforcement and Consequences

A breach of this Policy by a Member, Designation holder, officer, committee member, Governing Council member, employee, contractor or volunteer may constitute professional misconduct or a breach of the Code, and may result in disciplinary action in accordance with the Disciplinary Policy. Depending on the nature and severity of the breach, consequences may range from a formal warning through to suspension or revocation of membership or a Designation, termination of an engagement or volunteer role, and referral to a relevant regulatory or professional body, in addition to any civil or other legal remedies available to ISEA.

Where a breach involves an applicant for membership or a Designation, ISEA may reject or suspend the application, in accordance with the Membership Regulations and Member Admission Framework.

Relationship with Other ISEA Policies

This Policy should be read together with, and does not limit, ISEA’s other applicable policies, including:

  • the Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct, which addresses plagiarism, intellectual integrity and professional misconduct;
  • the Ethics Policy and Conflict of Interest Policy, which address broader ethical obligations and conflicts of interest;
  • the Membership Policy, Membership Regulations and Member Admission Framework, which govern membership eligibility, the Equity Research Assessment and the conferral, suspension and revocation of Designations;
  • the CPD Policy, which governs Continuing Professional Development requirements and accreditation;
  • the Privacy Policy, which governs the handling of personal data, including personal data that may be contained within a Submitted Research Report or Member Submission;
  • the Document Retention Policy, which governs the retention and disposal of ISEA records, including Submitted Research Reports;
  • the Complaints Policy and Disciplinary Policy, which govern the investigation and resolution of alleged breaches of ISEA requirements; and
  • the Whistleblower Policy, which establishes protected channels for reporting suspected wrongdoing.

These policies operate together and should be interpreted consistently. Where a matter is subject to a specific procedure established by one of these policies, that procedure shall apply in addition to the substantive requirements established by this Policy.

Artificial Intelligence and Machine-Generated Content

21.1 General Approach

ISEA recognises that artificial intelligence and other automated tools are increasingly used in the creation of research, educational and other content relevant to this Policy. This Section sets out general principles applicable to such content, in addition to the specific provisions in Sections 8.5 and 11.9.

21.2 Ownership of ISEA-Generated Content

Where ISEA, or a person acting on ISEA’s behalf, uses artificial intelligence or other automated tools to assist in developing Publications, Course Materials, CPD Materials or Website Content, ISEA shall own the resulting content on the same basis as if it had been created without the use of such tools, to the extent ownership of such content is capable of vesting in ISEA under applicable law.

21.3 Accountability for Machine-Assisted Content

The use of artificial intelligence or other automated tools by any Relevant Person in creating a Submitted Research Report, Member Submission, Conference Material, or other content provided to ISEA does not diminish that person’s responsibility for the accuracy, originality and integrity of the resulting content, and does not excuse a failure to comply with the warranties, disclosure obligations or other requirements set out elsewhere in this Policy or the Code.

21.4 No Submission of Third-Party or Unlicensed Training Data

A Relevant Person must not use an artificial intelligence tool to generate content for submission to ISEA in a manner that incorporates material non-public information, confidential information, or a third party’s copyright-protected work, in breach of Section 11.5, Section 12.6 or Section 15.2.

Review and Continuous Improvement

ISEA shall periodically review this Policy to ensure that it remains appropriate having regard to:

  • developments in intellectual property law and practice across the jurisdictions in which ISEA and its Members operate;
  • developments in equity research, investment analysis and educational technology, including artificial intelligence;
  • changes to ISEA’s membership structure, Designations, or Equity Research Assessment process;
  • issues or disputes arising from the application of this Policy; and
  • the evolving needs of ISEA and the global equity research profession.

The Executive Secretariat, in consultation with the Ethics & Professional Standards Committee and such other committees as the Governing Council considers appropriate, shall recommend amendments to this Policy where appropriate, subject to approval by the Governing Council.

Effective Date

This Intellectual Property Policy takes effect on the date approved by the Governing Council.
